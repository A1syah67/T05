Energy use of Australian TVs, and what power costs

Choosing a TV is also choosing an electricity bill. The first four charts look at the energy labels of TVs sold in Australia: how star ratings relate to energy use, and which screen technologies use the most. The last chart steps back to the wholesale price of electricity, which has risen sharply since 1998.

More stars, less energy

Each dot is one TV model. Higher star ratings tend to go with lower energy use, though bigger screens at the same rating still use more.

Average energy by screen technology

Mean labelled energy use (kWh/year), all screen sizes. OLED TVs use the most on average.

Comparing like with like: 55-inch TVs

Fixing the size at 55″ narrows the gap: LCD and LED are almost level, OLED is still higher.

Which technologies earn which star ratings?

Number of TV models at each star rating, stacked by screen technology. LED models make up most of every rating, while OLED models cluster at 3–5 stars. Switch to shares to compare the mix across ratings.

Wholesale electricity prices, 1998–2024

The bold line is the average of the mainland states (Queensland, NSW, Victoria, South Australia); grey lines are individual states. Hover to compare a year. Snowy region (ends 2007) is not shown.
